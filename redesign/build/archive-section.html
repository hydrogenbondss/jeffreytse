EX-012019

Welcome Home

Curator/ Wil Aballe Art Projects/ Vancouver/ 2019

How does our sense of self come to inform the spaces we occupy — and how do the spaces we occupy inform our sense of self? Building on Steven Vertovec's concept of superdiversity, Welcome Home examined home as a fluid essence capable of shifting alongside ourselves. Three artists working in sculpture, performance, found objects, and sound installation reflected on their relationships to home: cycles of identity and self-acceptance, remembrance and belonging across cultural influences, and abstracted representations of interior and exterior space.

Featured work“Cube in Cube,” resin, plastic, and oil, 2017, by Olivia Qiu.

EX-022018

Finite Appearances

Co-Curator/ Wil Aballe Art Projects/ Vancouver/ 2018

The body has always been mobilised as a site where presence is both inscribed and erased. Finite Appearances brought together works by Kelly Holmes made from imprinted bodily traces — plaster casts of hands, soap moulds, video documentation of a snow angel, and a floor-scattered arrangement of plaster navels. The exhibition asked whether intimacy can be successfully represented in any medium, and whether the permanence of art inevitably contradicts an intimate act.

EX-042017

Insights Unseen and Unheard

Artist/ Hatch Art Gallery/ Vancouver/ 2017

Insights Unseen and Unheard was a group exhibition at Hatch Art Gallery in Vancouver. The show brought together artists working across photography, sound, and installation to examine questions of visibility and perception — what remains at the limits of what can be seen, heard, or communicated.

Jeffrey Tse contributed “Untitled, 2017,” a photographic series installed in a horizontal sequence across the gallery wall. One contribution among several — the work sits within a broader conversation the exhibition was already having about bodies, spaces, and what photographs actually record.

Contribution“Untitled, 2017” — Jeffrey Tse. Photographic series, horizontal install.

EX-052017

Resonance

Curator/ JCCAC/ Hong Kong/ 2017

Presented at the Jockey Club Creative Arts Centre in Hong Kong, Resonance brought together works exploring memory, identity, and emotional trace — the ways in which experience leaves marks that persist, evolve, and resurface. The exhibition featured abstract acrylic paintings on circular canvases, examining relational experience as a structure rather than an event: something that accumulates, distorts, and continues long after its origin.

PUB-012019

The Offender Behind the Lens: The Ethical Legacy of Beitler's Lynching Photograph

Author/ UBC Undergraduate Journal of Art History (UJAH)/ 2019

A study of the ethical legacy of the photographic record of racial violence — the position of the lens, the responsibilities of looking, and what it means to circulate an image whose original act was the crime it documents.

Cited in press Milwaukee Independent, 2020 — in coverage of the emotional strain of seeing images and video of anti-Black violence.

  • Citation Milwaukee Independent — “The crushing emotional strain of seeing images and videos of anti-Black violence” (2020)

PUB-022026

One-Ply Realism: On Toilet Paper as Material Culture and Infrastructure

Author/ ROLL CALL — Material Culture Archive/ 2026

A critical essay in the form of an archive: the claim that the most overlooked object of daily life encodes the invisible — supply chains, hygiene politics, colonial inheritance, and the economics of who gets what standard of living by default — and that an archive can make the argument only through accumulation and the formal weight of treating the mundane as evidence.
